# Lucky Kick third-person minigame

## Design brief
Play Kelly lining up six penalty shots. Aim at one of six goal cells, then stop a repeating low-to-high power marker inside its green zone. The player reads the meter, times the shot, sees its trajectory, and learns from low/high feedback. Accurate timing earns a goal. Every shot consumes one of six attempts; restart immediately from the results panel. Character movement and extra Luck shots are outside this six-shot minigame.

## Core loop contract
Select a cell with touch, mouse or keys 1–6/arrows, then press Shoot or Space while the moving marker is green. A green shot reaches the selected cell; low power travels low and wide, high power clears the crossbar. Each attempt updates the six-slot history and goals counter. Flight locks input; the sixth landing opens results and restart.

## Encounter plan
A fixed third-person camera behind the existing Kelly idle model faces an 8m × 3.6m goal with three columns and two rows. Six projected buttons correspond to six world-space target cells. The meter repeats every 1.8 seconds with a 42–62% success band; no time pressure before shooting. Six equal-difficulty attempts form one event. Mobile camera backs out to preserve the whole goal. Simulation pauses while the tab/page is hidden. Ball flight uses a deterministic interpolated arc rather than a physics engine; frame delta is capped at 50ms.

## Validation
`node tools/check-kick-ball.mjs` covers timing boundaries, low/high misses, all attempts counting, input locking, six-shot finish, score and reset. `node tools/check-web-entrypoints.cjs` checks all linked scripts and the existing config fingerprint.

## Drag aiming update
Drag the field with mouse or touch to move a reticle across the goal, with a highlight for its current cell. Aim is clamped inside the posts and captured when shooting. Score is 10 per landed goal; misses give zero. Power is captured on pointer-down from the last rendered meter position. Green shots cross the goal plane exactly at the reticle and settle inside the net, remaining visible for 650ms before the next ball. Low shots fall short; high shots clear the bar. Tests cover every goal cell and both green boundaries, drag extremes, frozen shot aim, scoring on arrival and duplicate-settle protection.

## Camera aiming correction
Dragging now rotates the actual third-person camera in yaw/pitch. The reticle stays fixed at screen center; its camera ray intersects the goal plane to determine the shot aim and highlighted cell. Clicking a numbered cell or using keyboard selection also points the camera at that cell. Resize preserves the selected world aim. Rotation limits retain aim inside the posts. Camera tests verify quaternion changes, moving world projection, centered aim, all six targets, and desktop/mobile limits.

## Orbit camera and circular targets (v6)
Replaces the constrained look camera with a shoulder-offset orbit around Kelly at (-0.15,1.45,3). Drag can rotate through the full 360 degrees; pitch limits keep the camera above the ground. Orbit changes camera position and orientation together, retaining radius. The center ray intersects the goal plane only when the camera faces it; looking elsewhere gives no selected target. Numbered target selection and keyboard shortcuts move the orbit to a mathematically aligned shooting view. Resize retains the orbit and center aim.

The goal no longer has rectangular subdivision bars or rectangular reward overlays. Six world-space concentric circular bullseyes (red/cream/gold, radius 0.72m) are placed at the previous six target centers. Hit testing uses circular distance with the ball's 0.2m radius deducted, so the whole ball must fit inside the target. Green power with an off-circle aim cannot award points. Completed bullseyes disappear and cannot score twice; misses remain available. Six attempts, score feedback and reset behavior are unchanged. Tests verify the circular edge and outside-square-corner cases, every target's flight intersection, six-attempt limits, orbit position changes, front/back views, full rotation, pitch safety, center alignment and responsive resizing.

## Physical football polish (v7)
Replaces the interpolated sine arc with a dedicated one-ball physics simulation. Custom arcade physics is used here because the scene needs only a sphere, fixed primitive boundaries and authored target timing; no general rigid-body library is required. Simulation runs at 120Hz with a clamped accumulator. Analytic gravity/air-drag integration supplies initial kick velocity solved for the selected target. A continuous goal-plane crossing determines scoring, rather than a fixed timer. The ball bounces on the ground, loses energy to restitution and rolling friction, spins with its velocity, deflects from three frame capsules, and is caught by soft rear/side/roof net planes. Goal-plane checks are swept; posts use short fixed steps, rather than general-purpose CCD.

Visuals: actual truncated-icosahedron football with 20 light hexagons and 12 dark pentagons, recessed seams, angular rotation separate from ground-aligned contact compression, height-dependent contact shadow, subtle fast-flight streak, localized damped net ripple, and a short kick preparation/follow-through. Kick, bounce, post and net sounds share the existing mute preference. The ball remains in simulation after scoring, then fades away and the next ball fades in. The six-shot cap, circular target scoring and orbital camera remain intact.

Validation: physical shots for all six targets at both green edges and center; gravity/drag; net, post/crossbar and ground contacts; ball never below the floor; rolling to rest; exactly one result event; 30/144fps determinism; reset; orbital camera regression. Football surface geometry was checked for 20 hexagons/12 pentagons and outward-facing normals. These are numerical and geometry checks; browser visual/audio playback was not verified in this pass.
